Chapter 1: What analytic philosophy is

Quick check Which philosopher invented modern quantificational logic in the Begriffsschrift (1879)?

Frege's Begriffsschrift introduced quantifiers and variables. Russell discovered a contradiction in Frege's later system and developed the logic further in Principia Mathematica.

Quick check Which statement best describes the relationship between analytic and continental philosophy?

The division is one of tendencies — style, relation to science, typical forms of work — not of subject matter, language or rationality.

Chapter 2: Arguments

Quick check “Every swan I have seen is white, so all swans are white.” What kind of argument is this?

It generalises from observed cases, so it is inductive. (It is famously not deductively valid: black swans exist in Australia.)

Quick check An argument has true premises and a false conclusion. What follows?

A valid argument cannot have true premises and a false conclusion. So any argument that actually does is invalid.

Quick check An argument has false premises and a true conclusion. What follows about its validity?

Validity rules out only true premises with a false conclusion. False premises with a true conclusion are compatible with both validity and invalidity.

Quick check “Abortion is the killing of an innocent human being, so it is wrong.” Which unstated premise makes this valid?

The conclusion follows validly with “All killing of innocent human beings is wrong”. (Critics typically challenge either this premise or the claim that a foetus is a human being in the morally relevant sense.)

Quick check “Professor Jones says free will is an illusion, but she would say that — she's a determinist.” Which fallacy is this?

It dismisses the claim by pointing to features of the person who makes it, rather than engaging with her reasons.

Chapter 3: Propositional logic: language and translation

Quick check What is the main connective of ¬(p ∨ q) ↔ (¬p ∧ ¬q)?

The ↔ is the only binary connective outside all brackets. The formula is a biconditional.

Quick check What is the main connective of ¬(p → q) ∨ r?

By convention ¬ takes the smallest formula after it, here (p → q). The disjunction joins ¬(p → q) and r.

Translate It is raining but the match is not cancelled.

Key: r: it is raining; c: the match is cancelled

Model answer: r ∧ ¬c. "But" is conjunction.

Translate It is not the case that both it is raining and the wind is blowing.

Key: r: it is raining; w: the wind is blowing

Model answer: ¬(r ∧ w). The negation has the whole conjunction in its scope. Equivalent (by De Morgan): ¬r ∨ ¬w.

Translate You will get the job only if you have a degree.

Key: j: you get the job; d: you have a degree

Model answer: j → d. "Only if" introduces the consequent. Having a degree is necessary for getting the job.

Translate You will get the job if you have a degree.

Key: j: you get the job; d: you have a degree

Model answer: d → j. "If" introduces the antecedent.

Translate Having a ticket is necessary for boarding the train.

Key: t: you have a ticket; b: you board the train

Model answer: b → t. A necessary condition is the consequent: if you board, you have a ticket.

Translate The alarm sounds if and only if there is smoke.

Key: a: the alarm sounds; s: there is smoke

Model answer: a ↔ s.

Translate The picnic will go ahead unless it rains.

Key: p: the picnic goes ahead; r: it rains

Model answer: p ∨ r. Standard inclusive reading: if it does not rain, the picnic goes ahead. Equivalent to ¬r → p.

Translate Unless you revise, you will not pass.

Key: r: you revise; p: you pass

Model answer: ¬p ∨ r. "Unless B, A" is "A unless B": ¬p ∨ r, equivalently ¬r → ¬p, equivalently p → r (passing requires revising).

Translate Either the butler or the gardener did it, but not both.

Key: b: the butler did it; g: the gardener did it

Model answer: (b ∨ g) ∧ ¬(b ∧ g). Exclusive "or". Any equivalent form, such as ¬(b ↔ g), is accepted.

Translate Neither Jo nor Sam came to the party.

Key: j: Jo came; s: Sam came

Model answer: ¬j ∧ ¬s. Equivalently ¬(j ∨ s).

Translate Jo and Sam didn't both come to the party.

Key: j: Jo came; s: Sam came

Model answer: ¬(j ∧ s). "Not both": at least one of them did not come.

Translate If the government raises taxes and cuts spending, then unless the economy grows, unemployment will rise.

Key: t: taxes are raised; s: spending is cut; g: the economy grows; u: unemployment rises

Model answer: (t ∧ s) → (u ∨ g).

Translate You can have dessert only if you eat your vegetables, and if you eat your vegetables you can watch television.

Key: d: you can have dessert; e: you eat your vegetables; t: you can watch television

Model answer: (d → e) ∧ (e → t).

Chapter 4: Truth tables

Quick check If p is false and q is true, what is the value of p → q?

A conditional is false only when its antecedent is true and its consequent false.

Quick check Classify (p → q) ∨ (q → p).

If q is true, p → q is true; if q is false, q → p is true. Either way the disjunction is true. This is one of the "paradoxes" discussed at the end of the chapter.

Quick check An argument has premises that are jointly inconsistent. Is it valid?

There is no row in which all premises are true, so there is no counterexample row.

Quick check Which formula is equivalent to ¬(p → q)?

A conditional is false exactly when its antecedent is true and consequent false.

Chapter 5: Natural deduction for propositional logic

Quick check In the proof of p → (q → r) ⊢ q → (p → r) above, could line 7 cite line 4?

Line 4 lies inside the subproofs opened at lines 2 and 3, both of which are closed by line 7.

Quick check To use the premise p ∨ q to prove r, what should you usually do?

There is no rule that gets a single disjunct out of a disjunction. Proof by cases (∨E) is the standard way to use one.

Chapter 6: Predicate logic

Quick check Which of these is a sentence (has no free variables)?

In the second formula, x is bound by ∀x and y by ∃y. In the others, some occurrence of x is outside the scope of any quantifier on x.

Quick check Which translates “Only students who study logic pass” (domain: students)?

"Only" introduces the necessary condition: anyone who passes studies logic.

Quick check Which translates “Every student has read some book” on the reading where the book may differ from student to student? (Sx: x is a student; Bx: x is a book; Rxy: x has read y)

The universal comes first, so the book can depend on the student. The second option says one book has been read by every student. The third wrongly says that everything is a student.

Chapter 8: A taste of metalogic

Quick check Completeness says:

Completeness runs from ⊨ to ⊢. Soundness is the converse. "Every sentence or its negation is provable" is a different property (sometimes called negation-completeness), which Gödel's incompleteness theorem shows fails for arithmetic.

Chapter 9: Beyond classical logic

Quick check In Kripke semantics, which condition on the accessibility relation validates □A → A?

If every world can see itself, then whatever holds at all accessible worlds holds at the world itself.

Chapter 10: Language and meaning

Quick check Which of these expressions is a rigid designator, according to Kripke?

Proper names refer to the same individual in every possible world in which that individual exists. Descriptions like "the teacher of Alexander" can pick out different people in different worlds.

Chapter 11: Epistemology

Quick check What does a Gettier case show?

Gettier cases are cases of justified true belief that are not knowledge, so the three JTB conditions are not jointly sufficient.

Chapter 12: Metaphysics

Quick check On trope theory, what is the redness of this apple?

Tropes are particularised properties; different red things have numerically distinct but exactly resembling red tropes.

Chapter 13: Philosophy of mind

Quick check Which view best accommodates the possibility that an octopus and a human can both be in pain?

Functionalism defines pain by causal role, which can be realised in very different physical systems. This is the multiple realisability argument.

Chapter 14: Ethics and metaethics

Quick check Which theory says an act is right iff it is what a virtuous agent would characteristically do in the circumstances?

This is Hursthouse's formulation of a virtue-ethical criterion of right action.

Chapter 15: Philosophy of science and religion

Quick check Which inference is an instance of the quantifier shift fallacy?

Moving from ∀x∃y to ∃y∀x is invalid. The third option (∃y∀x to ∀x∃y) is valid, and the first is just ∀E.

Chapter 16: Paradoxes and puzzles

Quick check What does the first-order proof of Russell's paradox show about the barber who shaves all and only those who do not shave themselves?

The premise ∃y∀x(Sxy ↔ ¬Sxx) is refutable, so no such barber exists. (“The barber is a woman” evades the paradox only if the barber is outside the domain of men being shaved, which changes the premise.)

Translate Symbolise “If the repaired ship is the original, the reassembled ship is not” using r: the repaired ship is the original; s: the reassembled ship is the original.

Key: r, s

Model answer: r → ¬s. Any equivalent form, such as ¬(r ∧ s) or s → ¬r, is also correct.
