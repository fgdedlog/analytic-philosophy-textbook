Interactive tools

Fitch proof checker

Build a natural-deduction proof line by line and have every step checked against the rules of Chapter 5 (propositional logic) and Chapter 7 (quantifiers and identity).

#DepthFormulaJustificationStatusLine tools
Import / export as text

One line per proof line: leading | marks give the depth, then the formula, a semicolon, and the justification. An optional first line % premises ⊢ goal sets the goal.

How to use it

  1. Pick an exercise, or type premises and a goal and press Start. Premise lines (justified PR) are filled in for you.
  2. Type each line’s formula and its justification, e.g. →E 1, 3 or ->E 1,3. Press Enter to move from formula to justification, and Enter again to add a new line.
  3. To make an assumption, press + New subproof (or set a line’s justification to AS and indent it with ▸). Every line of the subproof keeps that depth. To close a subproof, outdent the next line with ◂.
  4. Press Check proof. Correct lines get a ✓; wrong ones get a ✗ with an explanation. The proof is complete when every line is correct and the last line is the goal at depth 0.

Your current proof is saved in this browser automatically (only on this device).

Rules the checker knows

Cite single lines with numbers and subproofs with ranges, e.g. ∨E 1, 2-4, 5-7. Rule names can be typed in ASCII (&I, vE, ->I, ~E, <->E) and common aliases are accepted (MP for →E, CP for →I, RAA for ¬I or IP, EFQ for X).

PR premise (depth 0, at the start)
AS assumption (opens a subproof)
R m reiteration
∧I m, n · ∧E m
∨I m · ∨E m, i–j, k–l
→I i–j · →E m, n
↔I i–j, k–l · ↔E m, n
¬I i–j (assume A, reach ⊥, conclude ¬A)
¬E m, n (A and ¬A give ⊥)
X m ex falso: from ⊥ anything
IP i–j indirect proof: assume ¬A, reach ⊥, conclude A
DNE m ¬¬A gives A
Derived: MT m, n · DS m, n · HS m, n · DNI m · DeM m · TND i–j, k–l
∀E m · ∀I m (name must be arbitrary)
∃I m · ∃E m, i–j (name must be new)
=I (no lines) · =E m, n · CQ m (derived)

What is and is not supported

  • Fully supported: the complete propositional system of Chapter 5, the derived rules listed above (which can be switched off for “basic rules only” practice), and the quantifier and identity rules of Chapter 7, including the restrictions on ∀I and ∃E.
  • Formulas in proofs must be sentences: no free variables. Names are lower-case letters a–s; variables are t, u, w, x, y, z. Predicates are capital letters followed directly by their arguments: Fa, Rxy. Write ∀x / ∃x (or forall x / exists x).
  • Not supported: function symbols, modal logic, and truth trees. The checker checks proofs you write; it does not find proofs for you.
  • The checker is strict about the exact form of formulas, but it ignores redundant brackets: (p ∧ q) and p ∧ q are treated as the same formula.
