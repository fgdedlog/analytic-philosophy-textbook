Further reading

A short, selective list of well-known works for each chapter. Classic papers are widely reprinted in anthologies; ask your library which editions it holds. Starred items (★) are accessible starting points.

The free online Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy has reliable, regularly updated entries on almost every topic in this book, and is the best next step after each chapter.

General introductions

  • ★ Bertrand Russell, The Problems of Philosophy (1912).
  • ★ Thomas Nagel, What Does It All Mean? (1987).
  • ★ Simon Blackburn, Think (1999).
  • Scott Soames, Philosophical Analysis in the Twentieth Century, 2 vols (2003).

Chapter 1: What is analytic philosophy?

  • Gottlob Frege, The Foundations of Arithmetic (1884).
  • Bertrand Russell, “On Denoting”, Mind (1905).
  • G. E. Moore, “A Defence of Common Sense” (1925).
  • Ludwig Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921) and Philosophical Investigations (1953).
  • Michael Beaney (ed.), The Oxford Handbook of the History of Analytic Philosophy (2013).

Chapter 2: Arguments, validity and fallacies

  • ★ Patrick J. Hurley, A Concise Introduction to Logic (many editions).
  • ★ Irving M. Copi, Carl Cohen and Kenneth McMahon, Introduction to Logic (many editions).
  • ★ Anthony Weston, A Rulebook for Arguments (many editions).

Chapters 3–7: Propositional and predicate logic

  • ★ P. D. Magnus and Tim Button, forall x: Calgary (an open-access textbook, freely available online; the proof system in this book follows its Fitch-style conventions closely).
  • ★ Wilfrid Hodges, Logic (Penguin, 1977; 2nd edn 2001).
  • Paul Tomassi, Logic (1999).
  • David Bostock, Intermediate Logic (1997).
  • Theodore Sider, Logic for Philosophy (2010).
  • Jon Barwise and John Etchemendy, Language, Proof and Logic (with Dave Barker-Plummer; 2nd edn 2011).
  • Peter Smith, An Introduction to Formal Logic (2nd edn 2020; truth trees).

Chapter 8: Metalogic

  • George Boolos, John Burgess and Richard Jeffrey, Computability and Logic (5th edn 2007).
  • Geoffrey Hunter, Metalogic (1971).
  • ★ Ernest Nagel and James R. Newman, Gödel's Proof (1958).
  • Peter Smith, An Introduction to Gödel's Theorems (2nd edn 2013).

Chapter 9: Beyond classical logic

  • ★ Graham Priest, An Introduction to Non-Classical Logic: From If to Is (2nd edn 2008).
  • Theodore Sider, Logic for Philosophy (2010), chapters on modal logic.
  • Saul Kripke, “Semantical Considerations on Modal Logic”, Acta Philosophica Fennica (1963).
  • Susan Haack, Philosophy of Logics (1978).
  • JC Beall and Greg Restall, Logical Pluralism (2006).
  • David Lewis, Counterfactuals (1973).

Chapter 10: Language and meaning

  • Gottlob Frege, “On Sense and Reference” (“Über Sinn und Bedeutung”, 1892).
  • Bertrand Russell, “On Denoting”, Mind (1905).
  • P. F. Strawson, “On Referring”, Mind (1950).
  • Saul Kripke, Naming and Necessity (lectures 1970; book 1980).
  • Hilary Putnam, “The Meaning of ‘Meaning’” (1975).
  • H. P. Grice, “Logic and Conversation” (1975).
  • J. L. Austin, How to Do Things with Words (1962).
  • A. J. Ayer, Language, Truth and Logic (1936).
  • ★ William G. Lycan, Philosophy of Language: A Contemporary Introduction (many editions).

Chapter 11: Epistemology

  • René Descartes, Meditations on First Philosophy (1641).
  • David Hume, An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding (1748), §4.
  • Edmund Gettier, “Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?”, Analysis (1963).
  • G. E. Moore, “Proof of an External World” (1939).
  • Alvin Goldman, “What Is Justified Belief?” (1979).
  • Robert Nozick, Philosophical Explanations (1981), ch. 3.
  • W. V. O. Quine, “Two Dogmas of Empiricism”, Philosophical Review (1951).
  • Nelson Goodman, Fact, Fiction, and Forecast (1955).
  • Timothy Williamson, Knowledge and Its Limits (2000).
  • ★ Duncan Pritchard, What Is This Thing Called Knowledge? (many editions).

Chapter 12: Metaphysics

  • W. V. O. Quine, “On What There Is”, Review of Metaphysics (1948).
  • J. M. E. McTaggart, “The Unreality of Time”, Mind (1908).
  • David Lewis, On the Plurality of Worlds (1986).
  • David Lewis, “Causation”, Journal of Philosophy (1973).
  • Peter van Inwagen, An Essay on Free Will (1983).
  • Harry Frankfurt, “Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility”, Journal of Philosophy (1969).
  • P. F. Strawson, “Freedom and Resentment” (1962).
  • Derek Parfit, Reasons and Persons (1984), Part III.
  • D. M. Armstrong, Universals: An Opinionated Introduction (1989).
  • ★ Earl Conee and Theodore Sider, Riddles of Existence (2005).
  • ★ Peter van Inwagen, Metaphysics (many editions).

Chapter 13: Philosophy of mind

  • Frank Jackson, “Epiphenomenal Qualia”, Philosophical Quarterly (1982).
  • John Searle, “Minds, Brains, and Programs”, Behavioral and Brain Sciences (1980).
  • Alan Turing, “Computing Machinery and Intelligence”, Mind (1950).
  • David Chalmers, The Conscious Mind (1996).
  • Jaegwon Kim, Mind in a Physical World (1998).
  • ★ Jaegwon Kim, Philosophy of Mind (many editions).

  • Judith Jarvis Thomson, “The Trolley Problem”, Yale Law Journal (1985).
  • J. J. C. Smart and Bernard Williams, Utilitarianism: For and Against (1973).
  • Peter Geach, “Assertion”, Philosophical Review (1965).
  • ★ James Rachels, The Elements of Moral Philosophy (many editions).
  • ★ Alexander Miller, An Introduction to Contemporary Metaethics (2003; 2nd edn 2013).

Chapter 15: Philosophy of science and religion

  • Thomas Kuhn, The Structure of Scientific Revolutions (1962).

  • Bas van Fraassen, The Scientific Image (1980).
  • Larry Laudan, “A Confutation of Convergent Realism”, Philosophy of Science (1981).
  • ★ Samir Okasha, Philosophy of Science: A Very Short Introduction (2002).
  • Anselm, Proslogion, chs 2–4, with Gaunilo's reply.

  • William Rowe, “The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism”, American Philosophical Quarterly (1979).
  • J. L. Mackie, The Miracle of Theism (1982).

  • Robert Nozick, “Newcomb's Problem and Two Principles of Choice” (1969).
  • Wesley Salmon (ed.), Zeno's Paradoxes (1970).
  • ★ Michael Clark, Paradoxes from A to Z (many editions).
